RESEARCHER SETTINGS
Unchecked = faithful to Lodge 1981 (no looking back).
Off by default. Line, number and area are the working modalities; loudness needs acoustic calibration before its numbers mean anything.
Without this, number is always second and "the second modality degrades" cannot be told apart from "number degrades".
The neutral middle is supplied (line of fixed length, the number 50, a fixed square). Removes the reference step entirely — halves the inventory — and removes the clipping and confusion that respondent-set references caused. Deviates from Lodge 1981 direct scaling, where opinion references were respondent-produced; logged as such.
Applies to the reference step of each inventory item and to the anchor trial of each calibration block — the moments a respondent has to actually attend to the standard. It does not guarantee attention, but it stops the anchor being clicked past in half a second.
Lodge's interview schedule printed the item, then "Reference Line", then "Response Line" on the same page — a new reference per question, always in view. Unchecking gives one shared reference per modality (our earlier design), which relies on memory.
Lodge showed nine stimuli once each, so the reference was never more than nine pages back. Doubling to 18 stretches memory further than his design ever did; this splits A and B into two halves of nine with the reference redisplayed between them.
Off by default: Lodge & Tursky's survey used line production + numeric estimation only. Each extra modality adds 10 productions.
On by default. The VAS asks the same question with the same anchors as the 1–5 scale but without fixed steps, so the pair separates discretization from compression. It is also the only format whose range can represent an uncompressed respondent: departure from the midpoint spans ~50:1, against 2:1 for the 1–5 scale. Order of the two rating pages is randomised per session and recorded.
Default is magnitude first. Rating on a category scale first gives respondents a category to translate, which contaminates the magnitude responses; this toggle exists so the order can be counterbalanced across participants.
Off = left-anchored (a shared origin, like drawing from a page margin). On = shapes grow symmetrically about the screen centre, removing the endpoint-position cue. Lodge does not specify placement.
Lodge 1981 presented each of the nine stimuli once (he notes stimuli would "more reasonably" be shown twice each). Doubling gives real test–retest estimates for the two primary blocks at a cost of ~3 minutes.
On by default. Direction and the category scale are collected in separate phases, and 3–5 items per respondent have contradicted across the pilots; handling them more than doubled Pearson agreement. Unchecking only removes the question — the contradictions are still detected and exported, with resolved: null, so the data is never quietly zeroed.
Off by default. "Block 1 of 6", "Line 4 of 10" and the modality name are useful while piloting, but a participant needs none of them to answer, and they sit above the thing they are supposed to be looking at.
Checked = Lodge 1976/1981 default: the standard is assigned 50 and respondents judge in proportion to it (loudness reference is still set at a "comfortable" level, as in 1976 — a loudness cannot be assigned by number). Unchecked = free modulus (the 1981 footnote-6 variant): respondents pick their own reference values, which reduces regression bias but risks awkward anchors. Either way this applies to calibration only — in the opinion task the references are always produced by the respondent, as in 1981 direct scaling.
White broadband is closest to Lodge et al. 1976 ("broad band noise", TDH-39 earphones). Pink is gentler but weights energy toward low frequencies.
~50–66 calibration trials, roughly 17–26 minutes depending on the doubling toggle. Lodge's two-modality calibration took 6–8 minutes. Expected exponents, for the researcher only (never shown to participants): line/number 1.0, area ≈0.7, loudness ≈0.67.
CALIBRATION

Practice making proportional judgments

Six short blocks. In each, the first item is your reference — give it any value that seems appropriate. Then respond in proportion to it.

Twice the reference → twice the value; ten times → ten times; half → half. No limit on the range you may use. Any value except zero.

Judge by eye, not by measuring. We want to know how big things look to you. Please do not measure the screen with your fingers, count anything, or work sizes out in your head. We are interested in your perceptions.
